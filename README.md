# loch.js — cross-platform POC

One TypeScript / Three.js frontend, built as an offline PWA with Vite and packaged for Windows, Linux and macOS with Tauri 2. LOX, Survex `.3d`, and Compass `.plt` files are parsed locally in the browser; no upload or backend. Drag/drop or open a supported file; orbit with left drag / one finger, pan with right drag / two fingers, zoom with wheel / pinch. Use the menu controls and altitude legend. Includes a generated native-format demo cave.

The startup demo keeps the menu bar expanded. On a computer (mouse, window at least 900 × 600 px) the menu stays open under the top bar, the dock's icons stack in a column under it, all three opaque, framed alike and lined up on the left edge, and the scale sits centred at the top edge. On a phone turned on its side, the top bar runs down the left edge, its six buttons in a narrow two-column block (Menu and Search, Underlay and Surface, Vertical section and Walls) above the indicators, and the dock down the right edge (icons upright), and the scale stays centred at the top. Controls live in a dock centred at the bottom of the screen: Field objects and SHP files; My location (GPS), Record object and Measure; Entrances with their label button; and the station label button. The Menu button sits in the top left corner with Search and Vertical section to its right and Underlay, Surface and Walls under them (files open from File → Open…, on iPhone straight into Files); and opens the **File**, **Scene**, **View**, and **Rendering** menus just below the bar; with the menu open, the model title and its description (the status line, wrapped over as many lines as it needs) appear at the bottom of the screen above the dock. The menu starts closed; the status line also appears on its own while a file loads or when loading fails. The red bounding box (Scene → Visibility → Bounding box) appears for 3 seconds after a model opens and after each projection, compass, tilt or Walker switch, then hides. Use **File → Open…**, **Scene → Visibility → Bounding box / Centreline / Walls / Surface / Indicators**, **Scene → Centreline → Surface / Underground / Color / Splay / Duplicate / Stations / Label**, **Scene → Walls → Culling / Texture walls**, **Scene → Surface → Image overlay / Transparency / Opaque underside / Opacity**, **View → Walker / Autorotate / Perspective / Lock tilt / Set rotation centre / My location (GPS) / Plan / Profile / Fit**, and **Rendering → Light / Detail / Full screen**. Visibility, Centreline, Walls, Surface, and Light are expandable groups. Perspective toggles between perspective and orthographic projection without rotating the camera, changing its target, or changing tilt lock. Scale at the camera target is preserved: perspective always uses its natural 45° field of view and carries the scale in the camera distance, while the orthographic camera moves back along its line of sight behind the whole model so nothing is cut off, however far you zoom out. While the camera is under the terrain (below the surface at its own position, or below its lowest point beside it), the surface's underside turns earth brown (its relief still shaded) and, with Opaque underside (off by default), opaque instead of transparent, so a view up from the cave is told apart from a view down onto the terrain; from above ground, nothing is tinted, not even the backs of far slopes higher than the camera. Plan looks down the Z axis; Profile is a side view with Z vertical. Both enable Lock tilt automatically. Fit recenters and fits the model while preserving tilt, rotation, and tilt lock. Lock tilt holds the current inclination while allowing heading rotation, pan, and zoom; uncheck it to tilt freely. **Set rotation centre** (View menu; double click or double tap does it directly) turns the next click or tap on the model into the rotation centre: the nearest visible wall, surface point, or centreline shot within a few pixels under the cursor is picked, and the view pans smoothly so that point sits in the middle of the screen. Orientation, distance, projection, and tilt lock are kept, and a short-lived ring marks the new centre. Dragging while the mode is active does nothing; clicking empty space keeps the mode open, and Esc cancels it. **My location (GPS)** (View menu, or the pin button in the dock) shows the device position on models stored in S-JTSK (Therion `cs ijtsk`, identical to EPSG:5514 Krovak East North). The browser's WGS 84 fix is converted in the app with the Slovak EPSG "S-JTSK to WGS 84 (4)" datum shift (about 1 m) and the Krovak projection, then placed on the LOX surface terrain at that point; outside the surface it uses the GPS height. A blue dot marks the position (pinned to the screen edge when off-screen) with a small arrow showing where the phone points: the compass heading (iPhone asks for motion and orientation access on the first tap) plus 5.5° magnetic declination for Slovakia and the S-JTSK meridian convergence at your position, projected into the current view, a translucent disc shows the reported accuracy, and a line at the bottom gives accuracy, height and x/y in the model's system (cs ijtsk), or the distance when you are outside the model. The first fix pans the view to you when you are within 1 km of the model. Models in local coordinates are reported as not georeferenced. Location needs HTTPS (the GitHub Pages build or the installed PWA) and the browser's permission. GPS heights are kept as sea-level (≈ Bpv) heights: Safari on Apple devices already reports height above mean sea level (Core Location, EGM2008), while Android reports height above the WGS 84 ellipsoid, so the EGM96 geoid height for Slovakia (about 39–46 m, from a 0.25° table) is taken off there. Texture walls is off by default; enabling it replaces wall depth colors with a repeating limestone texture and subtle surface relief. Texture mapping uses model coordinates, so LOX files need no UV coordinates or extra full-mesh normal arrays. The same stone appearance applies to Automatic point previews and refined views, and the texture is included in the offline PWA cache. Centreline colors use altitude by default. The shared color scale uses only underground centreline and splay shots plus cave wall vertices; surface shots, terrain, and isolated stations do not expand its range. Model fitting still includes all geometry. See [texture asset and generation prompt](src/assets/README.md). Culling is off by default; enabling it hides back-facing triangles and corresponding preview points, using the source triangle winding. Rendering → Light provides Static (default), Follow camera, and Headlight modes. Static uses world-relative Bearing (0–360°, clockwise from north/+Y) and Clino (−90° below to +90° above). Follow camera uses camera-relative offsets: 0°/0° places the directional light at the camera, positive bearing rotates toward camera-right, and positive clino rotates toward camera-up. Each directional mode remembers its own angles during the session: Static starts at 27°/53°, Follow camera at 0°/0°. Headlight replaces directional and hemisphere lighting with a camera-mounted soft spotlight, brightest in the centre and fading toward the screen edges. Its cone adapts to projection, zoom, and viewport shape, with brightness independent of model distance. Bearing/clino are disabled in Headlight and restored when returning to a directional mode. Scene visibility, culling, and light choices carry over when opening another file. A new version installs itself in the background; a **New version · Reload** button then stays in the menu row until the page is reloaded (the open page keeps running the old version until then). Click `loch.js` for the About dialog: authors (Stacho Mudrák, Pavel Herich), version and build information.

The loaded project title appears at the upper left and remains visible with the menu collapsed. Both loaders use the first nonempty LOX survey title, then the survey name or filename if no title is stored. Long titles are shortened visually to fit the screen.

**Dock shortcuts** mirror menu controls and stay in sync with them. The Ω button shows cave entrances (Scene → Centreline → Stations → Entrance) as a white omega with a thin black outline. Entrance symbols are sized by zoom relative to the model and by real distance (see SHP layers). The label button next to it (a small Ω with index 1) cycles entrance labels: names, then altitudes, then off. Entrance names come from the station comment, which Therion exports use for the cave name, falling back to the survey name. The station label button (a small + with index 1) shows all survey stations (Stations → All) with numbers, then altitudes, then number@survey, and the fourth click hides the stations again. A small letter in the corner of a label button shows the active label (N name, m altitude, # number, S survey); clicking a label button while its symbols are hidden turns them on first. Entrance labels are set separately from station labels (Stations → Entrance label).

**Field objects** record what you find in the field. The pin-with-plus button in the dock, right of My location, opens a form that starts reading the device position at once (GPS, converted to S-JTSK like My location, with the terrain height from the model surface when available). **Pick in model** hides the form so you can tap any wall, surface point or centreline shot instead; the point keeps the model's coordinates, and **Use GPS** switches back. Diacritics never enter the app: they are removed from the name, note and team as you type. On saving, the name becomes a Therion survey id, lower case with underscores, followed by the actual save date and time from the device clock (`Sinkhole below ridge` saved 22.10.2026 12:25:48 → `sinkhole_below_ridge_26-10-22_12-25-48`). Add a note and one or more photos (on phones, Add photo offers the camera or the photo library). Objects are stored on the device (IndexedDB) and survive reloads and offline use; the browser is asked to keep this storage persistent. On S-JTSK models they appear as hollow orange diamonds (black edge, orange band, empty middle) with their names (Scene → Visibility → Field objects). **File → Field objects…** lists them; tap one (or Open) to view it read-only with **Show in model**, **Edit** and **Close** (the list opens without jumping into the Surveyed by field); tap **Edit** to change its name, note, photos and position (the save time in its id stays), tap a photo to see it full size with ‹ › between photos, delete with Delete tapped twice, and **Export to Therion** saves `j_mobil.zip` exactly like the GPX → Therion j_mobil generator: `j_mobil/<id>/<id>_th.th` (entrance station `gps_mob` fixed in the model's coordinate system, `cs ijtsk` x y with Bpv height, note as comment, the Surveyed by field as team, `unknown` when left empty; the field starts empty), `thconfig_200.th` from the same template, `_data/desktop.ini` with the photos, plus `j_mobil.th` and `thconfig.th`. Objects without a position are left out of the export.

**In the field.** Every object card (entrance, SHP point, field object) has **Navigate**: it turns on GPS (and the compass) and shows a panel with the target's name, horizontal distance, height difference and true bearing, and an arrow turned from where the phone points (without a compass, the arrow points the way on the map as shown); × ends it. The last model opened from a file is kept on the device and reopens at the next start, also without a signal; **File → Forget saved model** returns to the sample cave. A lost GPS signal no longer turns location off: the app keeps waiting for the next fix.

**View → Vertical section…** asks for two taps on the model (wall, surface or centreline). Only a vertical slab around the plane through them, from a little before the first point to a little past the second (10 %, at least 10 m), stays visible (walls, surface, centreline, preview points, and the symbols and labels in it), and the view turns level to look straight across the plane, the first point on the left, so passages and terrain show as a profile along the section line. The panel gives the line's length and bearing, a width slider (1–500 m, 20 m to start), **Flip** to look from the other side, **Export** (a 1:1000 SVG profile drawing with terrain, centreline shots, named entrances and a chainage/height grid; a PNG image of the view; or the stations in the cut with chainage and height as CSV), and **End**. The section button in the top bar starts and ends it as well. Opening another model ends the section; for a true-scale profile switch to Ortho.

**GIS formats.** Besides shapefiles, **GPX** (waypoints, track and route points), **KML/KMZ** (placemark coordinates), **GeoJSON** (any geometry's vertices; WGS 84, or S-JTSK when its `crs` names EPSG:5514) and **CSV/TXT** with a header (`x;y;z` or `lat,lon`, comma, semicolon or tab, decimal commas, JTSK `y;x` order recognised, an optional name column) open as point layers like SHP. **File → Export points…** writes cave entrances, all survey stations, field objects or an imported layer as GeoJSON (S-JTSK EPSG:5514 or WGS 84), a zipped PointZ shapefile in S-JTSK (with .prj and UTF-8 .dbf), KML, GPX waypoints or CSV (x y z and lat lon).

**Smooth surfaces.** With Image overlay off, every surface (from the LOX or a terrain model) is shaded smoothly as in Loch, not triangle by triangle: each node's normal comes from the whole grid (central differences over about ±4 m, at least one node), so neither facets nor seams between the surface's meshes show; a mirrored grid's normals are turned to the side its cells face. **Terrain (DMR 5.0).** **Scene → Surface → Get DMR 5.0 from ZBGIS…** opens ÚGKK's MAPKA (theme Terrain, export) at the model, where the area round the cave is exported as GeoTIFF (up to 400 km², 1 m cells, S-JTSK [JTSK03] + Bpv; ÚGKK offers no service that hands out heights directly). **Open terrain (GeoTIFF)…** (or File → Import TIF…, or drag and drop) then reads the downloaded ZIP or `.tif` in the app, without GDAL; the first, optional step is **Crop terrain**: a shaded preview of the whole file (cells without data dark) where a rectangle is dragged, then resized by its **sides** (grips in their middle; from the start the rectangle is the whole area, so its sides drag inward) or corners, or moved by dragging inside it; **Square** (off at every crop) keeps it square, within the file. The preview is drawn at its own proportions, so the pointer lands on the sides wherever the dialog is or its south-west and north-east corners typed in S-JTSK, whole metres (Enter or leaving the field applies one); Whole area keeps everything, and only the file's blocks inside the rectangle are read afterwards. The reader: a small GeoTIFF reader (classic TIFF and BigTIFF, strips or tiles, uncompressed, LZW or Deflate, horizontal or floating-point predictor, one band of integers or floats, NoData) goes through the file block by block, so even a large export never sits in memory whole. The app then asks, in one dialog of two columns, for the grid step (1, 2, 5, 10 or 20 m, each with its columns × rows; the finest this device draws smoothly suggested — about 5 million nodes on a computer, 1.5 million on a phone), averages the cells onto it, trims empty rows and columns and leaves cells without data (outside an exported polygon) as holes. JTSK03 is moved into the model's JTSK with the GKÚ shift grid `sk_gku_JTSK03_to_JTSK` (PROJ-data, © GKÚ Bratislava, CC BY 4.0; resampled every 2 km, within 4 cm of PROJ; 0.2–1.6 m in Slovakia), EPSG:5514 files are used as they are, other systems are refused. and for the **relief image size**: sizes in px for the part that holds data, from a quarter of the file's cell to eight cells a pixel, each with its pixel size and an estimate of the JPEG (larger than 70 MB, or than the device can draw, unavailable), plus any width typed in (the height follows); the size suggested for the area is chosen beforehand: quality 0.98 (about 0.39 bytes a pixel for this shading; an enlarged image packs tighter), the longer side 1 000–15 000 px and the file about 3–70 MB (up to 179 million pixels; on a computer also within about 22 million pixels a gigabyte of memory the browser reports, on a phone 16 million pixels and 8 192 px a side, Safari's canvas limit). A last step, **Relief image style**, shows previews of the cropped part of the terrain (read again from the file at about 320 px when the crop is much finer than the whole-file preview) in eight styles: **Classic hillshade** (one light from the north-west, 45° high), **Multidirectional** (chosen beforehand: light from 225°, 270°, 315° and 360°, as below), **Altitude colours** (a hypsometric tint over the cropped terrain's height range, deep blue low ground through cyan, green, yellow and brown to white heights, darkened by the multidirectional shading; each pixel coloured by its mean height), **Slope** (white flat ground to dark slopes of 55° and more: rock steps and edges), **Sky-view factor** (how much of the sky each cell sees within 15 m, along 16 directions at 1–15 m: hollows, shafts, sinkholes and gullies dark whatever the light; the file is shaded in strips of at least 256 rows with 15 m of neighbouring rows above and below, so strips join without a seam), **Contours** (every 10 m, each 50 m bolder, brown, over the multidirectional shading; drawn per pixel of the image from its mean heights smoothed twice over about 5 m, cells without data left out, and antialiased by the distance to the contour), **Swiss** (after Imhof: light from the north-west, 40° high, together with the multidirectional light, warm on the lit side and bluish in shadow, ground greyish green low to warm white high) and **Composite** (sky-view factor 45 %, slope 25 % and multidirectional shading 30 %: the most detail). The relief is always shaded from the file's own cells (1 m for DMR 5.0), strip by strip of the file, and only then reduced to the chosen size: each cell's shade is shared, by area, among the pixels it overlaps (any number of cells a pixel, so the size is met exactly: 21 × 15 km gives 15 000 px, 1.41 m a pixel), as `gdaldem hillshade` followed by an average resampling would. (Shading heights averaged to the pixels first showed the uneven averaging, 1, 2 or 4 cells a pixel, as a fine grid pattern.) A smaller area is drawn larger with smooth interpolation (and, for the suggested size only, larger again while it stays under 3 MB). A size the browser cannot make is tried again with half the pixels; above 70 MB the JPEG is encoded again at quality 0.94, 0.9 or 0.85. The image is drawn in strips (no full copy of its pixels besides the canvas) and its pixels fill the grid's cells exactly (east and north sizes may differ by the rounding): a multidirectional hillshade as `gdaldem -multidirectional` (after Mark 1992: light from 225°, 270°, 315° and 360° at 45°, each weighted by sin² of its angle to the slope's aspect, Horn's gradient) in a light paper-like grey, saved as JPEG. The terrain replaces the LOX surface of the model with that image as its base (as a LOX image: Image overlay, opacity and map layers over it work the same; with Image overlay off it is shaded smoothly like every surface; a terrain kept by version 1.0.2 gets its relief image from the kept grid when the model opens (from the GeoTIFF again for the full detail); the model's box grows to take it in), is kept on the device for that model and comes back when it is opened again; **Remove terrain** brings back the LOX surface. **Export terrain to Therion** asks for a name (as Save terrain as LOX: diacritics dropped and every space, or character a file name cannot have, turned into `_`; Enter saves) and saves `<name>.zip` with a folder `<name>` holding `<name>.th`, `<name>.jpg` and a `thconfig` (`source <name>.th`, `cs ijtsk`, `export model -output <name>.lox`; names with spaces would go in quotes, as Therion needs), so running `therion` in that folder makes `<name>.lox`: one `surface` block in `cs ijtsk` (JTSK03 is moved into JTSK by the app with the GKÚ grid, so the surface lies exactly as shown here), `grid` from the south-west node, `bitmap` with the relief image placed by its outer corners (pixel 0 0 at the south-west), and the heights inline, rows from north to south as Therion reads them, cells without data filled from their nearest neighbours (Therion needs a full grid; cropping to a rectangle inside the data avoids them), with the credit “Zdroj produktov LLS: UGKK SR”. (Therion 6.2 still wants PROJ's `sk_gku_JTSK03_to_JTSK.tif` for any S-JTSK system and downloads it when missing.) **Save terrain as LOX…** (File menu and Scene → Surface) writes the same LOX in the app under a chosen name (no diacritics; the name is also the model's title): an empty root survey, the grid (holes filled) and the relief image, as Therion writes them (checked against Therion 6.2: same grid and image placement, heights within 5 mm of its centimetre text); it opens here like any model (its legend from the terrain's heights; reopened at the next start) or in Loch. Otherwise the terrain is kept only in the browser's storage on this device (grid and image), tied to the model or, on its own, to the GeoTIFF. With no S-JTSK model open, the terrain opens on its own (see above).

**Map layers.** While a piece of the surface waits for sharper tiles it keeps the map it shows, and tiles that do not come leave that map (a coarser zoom) in place rather than white squares; a piece with no map yet first gets a quick one three zooms coarser (a few tiles), so no part of the view stays empty while the sharp tiles load. The map button in the top bar (or **Scene → Surface → Map layers…**) lays maps **over the LOX image**, each with its own opacity (5–100 %): one imagery or outdoor map at a time (the **ZBGIS orthophoto**, the **Freemap outdoor map** or the **Mapy.com outdoor map**, and **Mapy.com aerial**, listed apart under **Scene → Surface → Other map layers…** and in the map menu only while it is on; switching one on switches the other off) and, over it, the **geological map of Slovakia 1:50 000** (ŠGÚDŠ, 50 % at first); with Image overlay off they lie over the plain shaded terrain. Each layer costs one extra texture look-up per pixel, so viewing stays as fast; what grows is graphics memory and download. While a layer is on, its attribution (with links) sits at the bottom right of the map; on phones at the very bottom, centred under the dock, which moves up by its height. Maps are draped in pieces of about a kilometre (one per surface mesh, clamped at their edges so no seams show); their Web Mercator tiles are reprojected to S-JTSK pixel by pixel, and requests carry the page's address as referrer. **Freemap** follows its operator's terms: tiles `outdoor.tiles.freemap.sk/{z}/{x}/{y}` with `@2x`, `@3x` or `@4x` for the screen's pixel ratio, zoom 5–18 only, **loaded on demand** (once the view rests, only for the pieces in view, each at the zoom that matches its size on screen, nearest first, within 40 / 12 megapixels with farther pieces made coarser) and **never downloaded ahead or kept on the device** beyond the browser's HTTP cache, so it needs a signal; attribution “© Freemap Slovakia · © OpenStreetMap contributors · Elevation data sources” links to freemap.sk and the OSM copyright page. The **ZBGIS orthophoto** (ÚGKK SR, `zbgisws.skgeodesy.sk/zbgis_ortofoto_wms`) comes from a WMS but is used as a tile map: every Web Mercator tile (512 px) is one GetMap request for its area (EPSG:3857 when the capabilities offer it, else EPSG:4326, exact to a fraction of a pixel within a tile), for one layer only (the named root, else the layer that sounds like the orthophoto and not an index of sheets, else the first), so it is shown and used offline exactly like Mapy.com below. Switching it on asks how much to save, each choice with its size and number of tiles still to download: **Optimal** (as Mapy.com below: about 3 m/px over the whole surface, sharper near the survey stations), **Max** (its sharpest zoom, 18, about 0.2 m/px at 49° N, **over the whole surface**, with every zoom below: for a surface of about 10 × 10 km some 40 000 tiles and a few GB), or **Online only** (nothing saved); online the view still loads the ZBGIS orthophoto as sharp as it needs whatever was saved (the saved tiles limit it only without a signal), and on a terrain without survey stations Optimal says it has nothing to be sharper near; the choice is remembered and used again when the layer comes back with a model. Saving stops cleanly, keeping what it has, when the device storage is full; tiles are fetched eight at a time, lowest zooms first. **Mapy.com** aerial imagery and the outdoor map are shown the same way, on demand for the view at the zoom it needs, but their tiles are also **saved on the device** so they work without a signal: switching the layer on starts saving, in the background behind what the view needs and lowest zooms first, every zoom from the one that shows the whole surface up to about 3 m/px over the whole surface, and sharper tiles only near the survey stations: about 1.6 m/px within 1 km, 0.8 m/px within 250 m and 0.4 m/px within 100 m (for imagery zoom 16, 17 and 18; for the 512 px outdoor tiles one zoom less). Tiles already saved are skipped, and they are kept by service and tile, not by model: models in the same area share them, and the tiles of every model stay until **File → Forget saved maps**. While tiles are saved, a panel at the bottom right shows the progress (tiles done of all, percent, bar) and the layer's line in the map menu says how far it is or that all tiles are saved; pieces of the view still loading are counted there too. The map menu also shows what is saved on the device (MB of the browser's allowance, Mapy.com tiles per layer), and the app asks the browser to keep the saved maps when space runs low. A save over 25 MB (or any on mobile data) first asks, with the size (MB or GB) and number of tiles; declining shows the map online only. For Mapy.com (whose tiles cost credits) the view asks for no zoom beyond the sharpest saved on a piece; a layer shows nothing until it is ready (WMS layer names read, quality chosen), so no request goes out without its layer; without a signal a tile that was not saved is cut from the nearest coarser saved tile and enlarged. Mapy.com needs the user's own free API key (developer.mapy.com): it is asked for the first time and once more if Mapy.com refuses it, kept in the browser, and changed with **Scene → Surface → Mapy.com API key…**. The geological map is one image over the whole surface (4096 / 2048 px) from the ŠGÚDŠ WMS in EPSG:5514 (requests split evenly into parts of at most 1024 px, layer names read from its capabilities when allowed), kept on the device. Every image is checked to have arrived whole (a cut-off JPEG or PNG is asked for again, up to three times) and service errors are reported with their reason. The services must allow use from other sites (CORS); otherwise the message says so (telling no CORS header from no answer or a refused key) and the layer goes off. **Map files (MBTiles).** **Add map file (MBTiles)…** at the bottom of the map menu adds an offline raster map from the device (for example a zonation map of a national park), however large: it is copied into the app's private storage on the device (the origin private file system, written by a worker with progress at the map), so it is there at every start without picking it again, and indexed once without loading it into memory — a small read-only SQLite reader goes through the file in 16 MB chunks and notes where every tile's bytes lie, also across overflow pages, for both MBTiles layouts (a `tiles` table, or `map` + `images` with deduplicated tiles); the index is kept in IndexedDB. The map then is a layer like the others, over the LOX image and under the geological map, with its own opacity, its name and attribution from the file's metadata, clear where the file has no tiles (PNG and WebP keep their transparency), shown on demand at the zoom the view needs within the file's zoom range and read from the file a few slices per tile, entirely without a signal. Several files can be added; **Remove map file** (tap twice) deletes one from the device. Vector MBTiles (PBF) are refused with a message; when the browser cannot keep a copy, the picked file serves for that session. Opening another model switches all layers off; the layers that were on for a model (and their opacity) are remembered by its file name and size and come back when it is opened again, the geological map from the copy kept on the device.

**Measure** (ruler button in the dock) turns taps into measuring points. A tap snaps to an entrance, field object or SHP point within 16 px (when Ω shows them), to a station (when all stations are shown) or to your GPS position; elsewhere it takes the wall, surface or centreline point under the finger. The points are joined by a yellow line with each length; the card gives the last leg as distance and horizontal distance, height difference and inclination, and bearing on the S-JTSK grid and from true north (meridian convergence applied), plus the total for more legs. **Nearest to surface** finds the closest point of the model's terrain to the last point in 3D (searching the grid triangles around it, bounded by the vertical distance) and shows it as a dashed blue line with its distance, inclination and bearing, the horizontal distance and bearing to the nearest place where the terrain passes the point's own height (the contour at that height, a green dashed line: the shortest level way out to a slope), the vertical distance to the terrain straight above, and the surface point's x y z. A measured GPS position follows new fixes. Undo removes the last point, Clear starts again, and Esc or the ruler button ends measuring. A double click or double tap still sets the rotation centre while measuring and adds no point. All values are whole metres and degrees.

**SHP layers** add point data from GIS. Open a `.shp` (or GPX, KML/KMZ, GeoJSON, CSV) with the open button, File → Open…, or drag and drop, together with its `.dbf` (point names), `.prj` and `.cpg` when you have them. Points, multipoints, and the vertices of lines and polygons (2D, Z or M) become hollow coloured rings (black edge, coloured band, empty middle) at the file's own Z; points without a height are pinned to the model's terrain (or the model centre outside it). Coordinates are used as they are when in S-JTSK like the model (cs ijtsk / EPSG:5514); positive JTSK Y/X are flipped and WGS 84 (from the .prj or by value range) is converted. Names come from a NAME/NAZOV/ID-like field. Invisible control characters left in LOX texts by encoding mix-ups (such as U+009D after ť) are dropped when the file is read. The dock's entrance buttons also drive field objects and SHP points: Ω shows or hides all three, and its label button switches names, altitudes (terrain height) and no labels for all of them; importing a layer or saving an object turns Ω on. SHP labels that would overlap are skipped. While symbols are small (zoomed out, up to 70 % of full size), an entrance, field object or SHP point that would touch one already drawn hides behind it (entrances first, then field objects, then SHP points); the next one shows only where it touches none, and hidden symbols cannot be tapped. Entrance symbols, field objects and SHP dots are sized from the screen scale relative to the model (25 % with the whole model in view, 100 % from a 100 m scale bar, logarithmic in between). In perspective each symbol uses the screen scale at its own real distance from the camera, so nearer symbols are larger whatever the rotation centre; orthographic views use the one zoom for all. Layers are kept on the device; Scene → Visibility → SHP layers hides them. **File → SHP files…** (or the layers button in the dock) lists the layers at the top to pick from, steps through their points (‹ › and the point list, in the row above Show in model), shows each point's x/y, terrain height, file Z and all .dbf attributes, and **Show in model** centres it (zooming in to a 100 m scale if needed) and rings it; the ring moves to whichever point (SHP point, entrance or field object) you tap next, and a tap beside the points or Esc removes it; **Remove layer** (tap twice) deletes the layer. Positions taken in the app (GPS or picked in the model) are rounded to whole metres.

**Walker** starts off and can be toggled from View → Walker or the walking-person button in the top-right bar. Left drag looks around from the camera’s position (drag down to look up, drag right to look left), right drag pans, and middle drag / wheel moves forward or backward along the viewing direction. Movement has a fixed speed independent of orbit distance: 10 metres per 100 pixels of middle drag, and 2 metres per 100 wheel delta pixels. Panning uses 5 metres per 100 pixels. On touchscreens, one finger looks around, two-finger dragging pans, and pinching walks forward/backward at the fixed walking scale (spread fingers to move forward). Adding or lifting a finger does not jump the view. Camera tilt remains Z-up; Lock tilt also applies while walking. Both controls stay synchronized, and switching modes preserves the current view.

**Indicators** starts enabled under Scene → Visibility and controls the project title, altitude color legend, compass, tilt indicator, and horizontal scale bar together. They sit in one translucent bar across the top, its items spread evenly: Menu, Search and Vertical section on top, with Maps (map layers over the LOX image, each with its opacity, in a small menu), the Surface switch and the Walls switch (a cave passage; each struck through in red when off; the same as Surface and Walls under Scene → Visibility) under them; then compass, tilt, the vertical altitude legend (highest at the top) and the projection switch, which cycles Persp → Ortho → Walk (the original loch.js walker with helmet and lamp) → Persp. Models open from File → Open… or by drag and drop; so do terrain GeoTIFFs (a `.tif` or the ZIP from ZBGIS), also with **File → Import TIF…**: under an open S-JTSK model they become its terrain; with no model, or one in other coordinates (the sample), the terrain opens **on its own** as the whole scene (centred on it, named after the file, with its altitude legend, map layers usable), is kept on the device and reopens at the next start, so a Therion surface can be made from a GeoTIFF without any cave (**File → Export terrain to Therion**, also under Scene → Surface; Remove terrain empties the scene). On a computer the Menu button folds the menu row (File, Scene, View, Rendering) away and the tools below move up; a second click brings it back (it starts unfolded). **File → New** (closing the File menu) empties the scene (no model, sample, section, terrain or map layers; field objects and SHP layers stay, as they belong to the device) and forgets the saved model, so the app also starts empty until a model is opened. The scale is a thin line with five equal downward ticks (four parts) centred under the bar, its value on the same row to the right of the line. In perspective it is exact only at the depth of the rotation centre, so it reads ≈ and its line is dimmed; in orthographic views it holds everywhere. Click or tap an entrance, SHP point or field object to see its details in a card above the dock, as see-through as the top bar (name, station@survey, survey, altitude, x y, the SHP attributes, or the object's name, note and photo count with Open); this works whether or not labels are on. **Search** finds station numbers (every survey that uses the number is listed with its survey path; `12@krasna` narrows to matching surveys), cave names from entrance comments, and surveys (centred on their stations); tap a result to centre and zoom to it. Double click, or double tap, on a wall, the surface or a centreline shot to make it the rotation centre. The page itself never zooms on phones (no double-tap or pinch page zoom, form fields at 16 px). Indicator panels use translucent backgrounds; bearing runs clockwise from north (+Y), and tilt is 0° horizontal, +90° up, or −90° down. Click the compass to face north with the heading held (rotation keeps only tilt, pan and zoom); click again to return to the previous heading. The third indicator switches perspective and orthographic projection (a box drawn in perspective or in parallel projection, labelled Persp or Ortho). Click the tilt indicator for a level side view with tilt locked; click again to return to the previous tilt and tilt-lock setting. Each toggle restores only its own angle, so both can be combined, and Plan, Profile or Fit clears them. The scale bar follows zoom and projection changes, and uses a rounded metric distance measured in the plane through the camera target for perspective views.

**Centreline** has separate Surface, Underground, Splay, and Duplicate toggles; only Underground is enabled by default, so surface, splay and duplicate shots start hidden. The Visibility → Centreline checkbox still hides or shows the entire centreline. Color defaults to Altitude; Default uses pure white for ordinary underground and duplicate shots. Surface shots stay gray in either color mode. Splays use half saturation and lower lightness in Altitude mode, and muted gray in Default mode. Duplicate shots otherwise use the same colors as ordinary underground shots. Shots with multiple flags respect each applicable visibility filter; LOX shots marked not visible remain hidden. Both loaders preserve the shot types, and these controls retain their choices when another file is opened.

**Surface** displays terrain grids and their embedded JPEG/PNG map images from the LOX file, positioned using their own world calibrations. It starts visible, with Transparency enabled and Opacity at **50%**, matching [Loch’s defaults](https://github.com/therion/therion/blob/master/loch/lxSetup.cxx). Image overlay starts enabled. Turning it off replaces the map image with a 100% white terrain material shaded by the current scene lights, including Follow camera and Headlight. Turning it back on restores the image without reloading it. The Opacity slider appears when Transparency is enabled; turning transparency off makes the terrain opaque and preserves the slider value. These choices carry over to the next model. Terrain without an embedded image is white, and files without terrain display the cave as before. Terrain is included in the model’s bounding box and initial fit; cave depth colors retain their original cave altitude range. Both file loaders support surfaces, and Automatic detail composites transparent terrain once after the opaque wall batches finish.

**Stations** under Scene → Centreline is an expandable group with Entrance, Fixed, Continuation, and All, all unchecked by default. Entrance shows a black omega symbol matching the app icon in a white circle, Fixed a red filled triangle, Continuation a question mark, and All the small white station dots. Special symbols are centred on their stations; if several selected types apply, Entrance takes priority over Fixed, then Continuation. Surface, entrance, and fixed stations remain visible when their surface/underground centreline filter is off. Ordinary underground stations follow the Underground filter. **Label** defaults to **(None)**; choose **Number** for the stored station name, **Short** for `name@survey`, **Full** for `name@survey.parent.root`, **Altitude** for the original elevation in metres, or **Comments** for station comments (LOX and Compass). Labels use a single screen overlay without depth testing. Overlapping labels are skipped, considering commented stations first; zooming in reveals more labels. Long labels are shortened to fit. Labels follow the camera. Temporary splay endpoints (`.` and `-`) have neither dots nor labels, even when Splay lines are enabled. Clearing all station selections or turning off master Centreline visibility hides station markers and labels. Both loaders preserve station names and survey hierarchies, and choices carry over between files.

**Surface → Nearest layer only** (off at start; also the dock's surface button: with a transparent surface its first click turns this on, the second hides the surface and the third shows it again with all layers) draws a transparent surface in two passes, every piece into depth first and then the colour of the nearest surface only: slopes behind a ridge no longer show through it, and the order of the pieces (sorted one by one by distance) no longer makes a far slope suddenly jump in front of a near one as the camera moves, while the cave under the surface still shows through. One layer alone would look darker than the overlapping ones, so it is drawn as opaque as two layers of the chosen opacity (50 % shows like 75 %). Animation views keep this setting with Scene changes. **Rendering → Background** sets the view's background from black (at start, as in loch.js upstream) through greys to white, kept in this browser; on a light background (over 55 %) labels, the scale, field-object names, station dots and an uncoloured centreline turn dark. Images and films keep the view's background unless White background is chosen. **File → Save image…** (taken over from Stacho Mudrák's loch.js) asks for width and height in pixels and the format, **PNG** or **JPG** (quality 0.92), then renders the view at exactly that size in its own framebuffer, without touching the live view (a large model at full wall detail, batch by batch). Keep aspect ratio starts on. Indicators and labels starts at 100 %, keeping their proportion to the view height, so a larger image gets larger symbols and labels; higher percentages enlarge them further. Up to 16 384 px a side (or the graphics device's limit) and 128 megapixels. The image shows what the view shows: the model, terrain and map layers, entrance and other symbols, station labels, field objects, SHP points and measuring, the compass, tilt and legend in a panel at the top left, the scale at the top, map credits at the bottom right and the model's title at the bottom; menus, dock and status are left out. Without **White background** it keeps the view's own dark background; with it (for printing) the background is white, station dots and an uncoloured centreline dark, labels dark with a white edge and the indicators dark without panels. Where the browser offers it a save dialog picks the file name (no diacritics), otherwise the image is downloaded. **View → Animation…** opens the animation editor (also from loch.js upstream): **Mark** records the current camera (and, with Scene changes, the visibility, labels, centreline colour and terrain controls); select a view to go there, **Update** replaces its camera, **Edit…** sets its name, incoming transition duration, signed number of rotations, seconds per rotation, Walker mode and Transition view (passed through without stopping); views can be moved up and down or deleted. **Play** (or View → Play animation) starts from the selected view; a drag, the wheel, a menu change or Esc stops it. Loop adds the way back to the first view. Transition views join their neighbours with continuous speed and direction; ordinary views and rotations ease to rest. Animations are loaded and saved as Loch-compatible `.lxa` files (recognised by their `LochPresentation` root, also by File → Open or drag and drop), with camera coordinates in the model's own system; settings this app does not have (wall transparency, grid, legacy stops) stay in the XML untouched. While stopped, each view shows as a small red camera and a yellow line traces the camera's way, with the views' names as labels; they never appear in images or films. With **Scene changes** each view also keeps the display: walls, centreline (underground, surface, splays, duplicates, colour), surface (shown, transparent, opacity, image), bounding box, indicators, station dots, entrances and fixed points, labels — and, beyond Loch's settings, the map layers with their opacity, SHP layers and field objects (as `scene-lochjs-*` attributes Loch leaves alone). While the camera travels to the next view the surface fades from one view's visibility and opacity to the next (transparent while it does), map layers staying on change their opacity, those going off fade out over the first half of the way and those coming on fade in over the second half; switches change when the camera arrives. During playback and in films the map layers load for the moving camera about once a second (a film waits until they have loaded, at most 20 s). **Labels** in the editor pin a text with an icon (peak, cave entrance, spring, sinkhole, point or the text alone) to a place of the model: **Add label…** asks for a click on a wall, the surface, a centreline shot or a symbol (Esc cancels), then for the text, the icon, the views (shown from the arrival at the first until the camera reaches the view after the last, fading in and out over half a second; to the end after the last view) and the S-JTSK coordinates, which can be corrected by hand. With **Hide behind terrain** (on by default) a label on or above the ground fades out over 0.3 s while terrain stands between it and the camera (the way is sampled against the terrain heights every 5 m or so) and fades in again; labels in the cave, and all labels while the camera is underground or the surface hidden, always show. They show above everything else while the animation plays, in its MP4 and, with the editor open, for the selected view; they stay with their views when views are added, moved or removed, and are kept in the `.lxa` as `LochJsLabel` elements Loch leaves alone. **Caption** in the editor is the text at the bottom of the film instead of the model's name (kept in the `.lxa` as a `caption` attribute, which Loch leaves alone; empty: the model's name, a terrain opened on its own without the `.tif` of its file); the film's file is named after it too. Images and films are saved under that name without diacritics, spaces and characters files cannot have turned into `_`, and never with the GeoTIFF's `.tif` in it. Once views exist, a clapperboard button in the dock shows or hides the editor. Where the browser offers Document Picture-in-Picture (Chrome, Edge) the editor's **⧉** button moves it into a window of its own, which can stand beside or outside the browser window and stays on top (its dialogs open there too); **⇲** or closing that window brings it back. **Speed** (View menu and the editor, the same setting, remembered in this browser: 0.25× to 4×) sets how fast the autorotation turns (15 s a turn at 1×, so 7.5 s at 2×) and how fast animations play and are filmed: every transition and rotation is divided by it, while the `.lxa` file keeps its own durations. **File → Save animation (MP4)…** (also **Save MP4…** in the editor) renders the animation from its start into a silent **MP4** (one full loop with Loop on), or with no views one 360° turn round the rotation centre in the autorotation's direction and speed (15 s at 1×): width and height (even, Full HD by default), indicator and label size, FPS (1–120, 30 by default) and quality (Low, Medium, High). Every frame is rendered separately, so a slow device makes no film shorter or jerky. **Detail**: **As on screen** (chosen beforehand, fast) draws each frame in one pass with the walls the view itself would draw for that camera (Rendering → Detail: all of them at Full); **Full, batch by batch** draws every wall batch of a large model at full detail one after another (as many as fit in 50 ms at a time, their geometry kept on the graphics card for the whole film on a computer) — much slower for large models; progress shows the frames done, percent and time left, and Cancel or Esc stops it. The view, scene and camera come back as they were. Encoding uses WebCodecs through [Mediabunny](https://mediabunny.dev/) (H.264, else VP9 or AV1 in MP4), included in the offline app; which codecs and sizes work depends on the browser.

**Autorotate** starts off and can be toggled from View → Autorotate. It turns around the current orbit centre once every **15 seconds**, preserving tilt and distance, with speed measured from elapsed time rather than frame count. Start, stop, and start again within one second, measured from the first start, to reverse direction. The chosen direction is remembered for later starts and app reloads (rotation still starts paused). Dragging temporarily pauses the automatic turn. Loading/preparing a large file and hidden browser tabs also pause it; rotation resumes afterward. Pausing lets Automatic detail refine the stationary view as usual. While autorotation is enabled and the page is visible, the app requests a [screen wake lock](https://www.w3.org/TR/screen-wake-lock/) to prevent automatic screen sleep/locking where supported. Pausing or leaving the page releases it; returning to the page requests it again if autorotation remains enabled.

## Development

Requires Node.js 22+ and npm. `npm install && npm test && npm run dev`. Production site: `npm run build && npm run preview`. Serve the `dist` directory over HTTPS for PWA installation (localhost is also accepted). After first successful load, install the app via your browser and reopen offline.

Desktop: install Rust, platform [Tauri 2 prerequisites](https://v2.tauri.app/start/prerequisites/), and `npm install`. Run `npm run tauri dev`; bundle with `npm run tauri build`. Linux AppImage/DEB requires WebKitGTK 4.1 etc. Windows packages need WebView2 (provided or installed by Windows/installer). macOS builds need Xcode command-line tools.

Desktop builds use their bundled frontend without a PWA service worker. On launch, the native app removes any service workers and cached pages left by older desktop versions and reloads once if needed. Updating or reinstalling therefore recovers from the old desktop PWA cache automatically. Browser and Android PWA builds retain offline caching.

## CI and publishing

`.github/workflows/ci.yml` tests/builds web and the three desktop systems on PRs and pushes. `main` pushes deploy GitHub Pages (enable **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**); `v*` tags attach `.exe`, `.msi`, `.deb`, `.AppImage`, `.dmg` release assets. The macOS `.app` is delivered inside the DMG and stored as a workflow artifact. Unsigned desktop binaries may trigger operating-system security prompts. GitHub Pages path is inferred from `GITHUB_REPOSITORY`.

CI identifies builds as `<last-version>-<short-commit>`, using the latest reachable `v*` version tag without its `v` prefix and seven commit characters (for example `0.1-abcdef1`). Before the first version tag, the package version is used. Installer filenames end in that build ID, for example `loch.js_0.1.0_x64-setup_0.1-abcdef1.exe`. Artifact archives are named `desktop-<platform>-<build-id>` and `web-pwa-<build-id>`. Compare the short commit with the About dialog opened by clicking `loch.js`. Versions are numbered from 1.0.0 (package.json, tauri.conf.json, Cargo.toml), the patch number going up with each new version.

## LOX notes and limitations

The LOX parser reads Therion survey metadata, stations, centreline shots, indexed cave walls, surface terrain, and surface imagery. Source coordinates are locally recentered for floating-point precision. Small LOX walls use shared indexed vertex normals within each scrap; separate scraps do not share normals.

Survex `.3d` imports support [versions 3–7](https://survex.com/docs/3dformat-old.htm) and [version 8](https://survex.com/docs/3dformat.htm), including compressed station names, survey hierarchy, surface/duplicate/splay flags, and centimetre-to-metre coordinate conversion. [Compass `.plt`](https://www.fountainware.com/compass/HTML_Help/Compass_Viewer/plotfileformat.htm) imports convert North/East/Up coordinates in feet to East/North/Up metres, preserve station and survey names, and keep disconnected traverses separate. As in Survex’s Compass reader, plot-excluded `P`/lowercase `d` legs are classified as surface, `L` as duplicate, and `S` as splay. Feature records are skipped. Both imports provide centreline, station labels, and LRUD walls using the existing controls.

**LRUD walls** are generated when loading usable passage dimensions from LOX, Survex, or Compass. LOX section types (oval, square, diamond, tunnel), vertical thresholds, and no-LRUD flags are respected; stored scraps are preserved alongside explicitly requested LRUD walls. Survex passage boundaries and Compass move commands keep separate passages disconnected. Sparse Survex cross-sections interpolate along intervening underground survey legs. Surface, splay, duplicate, and hidden legs do not generate walls. Negative/missing dimensions stop at the station axis; wholly missing or zero-size cross-sections produce no walls. Shared profiles are welded around bends, with caps at passage ends and stable profiles for vertical shafts. Generated wall extents contribute to fitting and the underground color scale. The existing Walls visibility, culling, texturing, and lighting controls apply. These walls approximate passage tubes; complex branch junctions can overlap.

LOX files larger than 32 MiB use an experimental full-detail loader: a worker reads at most 8 MiB at a time, scans the bounds, and transfers batches of at most 65,536 triangles. It never reads the whole file into one buffer or expands large meshes into JavaScript point/triangle objects. Source triangle order and winding are preserved. Large walls use GPU flat shading and altitude coloring to avoid normal/color arrays; small files retain their existing shading. The bounds and centreline remain visible during loading, with the walls shown after geometry preparation and upload. Status progress continues through geometry preparation and GPU upload with batch percentages, then shows animated indicators for shader compilation and the first wall render. Loading finishes only when that GPU render completes. Opening another file cancels the current load and preparation.

**Rendering → Detail → Automatic** uses shaded point samples and a 262,144-triangle budget while moving. Point normals come from at most 512 sampled faces per batch, with altitude colors and the scene’s hemisphere/directional lighting; no full-mesh normal calculation is needed. After the camera stops, every visible triangle batch is progressively drawn into a cached color/depth image; the moving-preview budget does not limit the finished stationary view. Each batch's GPU buffers are released after drawing, while the full source geometry remains in memory. Moving the camera resets refinement. Loading and refinement show a background progress bar behind the status text. **Full detail** is the default (set again at every start, so a browser does not bring back Automatic from an earlier visit) and attempts to draw every triangle directly. `domica-10cm.lox` has 27,647,432 triangles and needs about 949 MiB for positions alone; direct full-detail rendering also needs a GPU copy and can be very slow on software graphics or devices with limited GPU memory.

Generated sample: `python scripts/generate-sample.py` — synthetic geometry in genuine LOX record layout, not a model exported by Therion. Test against actual Therion exports before production. No cross-platform runtime/browser checks, notarization, signing or release publication can be claimed until GitHub runners are executed.

**Reproducibility:** The package-lock could not be generated in the offline build environment. CI currently runs `npm install`; commit a lockfile and change this to `npm ci` once registry access is available.
